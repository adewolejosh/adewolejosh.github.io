### Landed, Ahh!

---

Here, at landing, at around 6pm, I met a (beautiful, I must add or say [*added this piece later on]) lady, whom I later found out, we went to the same school, different faculties, man, really small world it is and both of us were going to the exact same destination. Remember the person (who knew other people) I met earlier, he offered to guide me. Sadly, the lady I met and I had to split off here, we both would later continue our journies the next day, but I honestly just felt the need to do a little more that day and continue the next day. Thanks to my helper, I did go a bit further in my journey. This was indeed, the hottest journey I have ever experienced, here's a simulation - imagine driving at a very high-speed on a highway, of course you would expect a cool breeze going in and out, and maybe at some point you may want to wind up the glasses if you do feel cold or if you feel the breeze "a little too extra", but here, imagine the only breeze passing through is filled with completely hot air (let's assume ~35 degree celcius), now think about going like this for about 3 hours, no AC in the car, if you let the windows down, you are greeted with far worse air, so you wind up to avoid that air, crazy & brazy. We got into "town" (the city's capital infact and I must say - the best part of the whole location), safely after over 3 hours of driving (from 6pm to 9 ish) through somewhat like a dessert - vast running open fields, barely any civilization or habitation for the most part, sunsetting leading to darkness at some point, still with the hot-air and a burst of emotions. After some settling physically - which came after several roundabouts due several minor decisions, my helper helped look for a lodge that night for me (to rest my head and begin the next day). ### No way, What?

---

I had a little story on how all these would go, but nothing and I mean, absolutely nothing, on God's created earth, in any way, would prepare you for any of this. I woke up as early as I could, maybe 3/4 am, set out to have a bath and go back to bed for some minutes (also after having some personal time), everything was tiring. As the day break, nothing prepared me for the level of exposure to the Sun, I had; not even the hot-air of the previous day (evening), could have prepared me for that. The Sun was so intense, I don't think its purpose was to give Vitamin D, or even less worse, to make you break a sweat, it felt like its aim was to make you BURN! and I mean it, and you won't believe, natives said, we all arrived at the "most" wonderful time of the year (of course this wasn't Christmas, lol), referring to the fact that this was a rainy season, and the weather is much more "friendly" (sounds like an allegedly kinda statement to me), than it would be during the dry seasons. All these, coupled with a compulsory uniformed dressing, which was thick! with a head covering barely enough to shield you and in a very open area where there is barely any shade. Open area - speaking on this – I didn't pay attention much to nature before, if I'm being honest; cause it was then (here) I realized, my home had way more clouds high up in the skies, protecting one in some way from the Sun, but over "here", a cloud is a big blessing and priviledge, or so I did see it. It was almost like, if you even saw a cloud in the sky, you could almost say the rain was going to fall; Interestingly, all through my stay of 20 days, rain only fell twice, several (maybe 3 or 4) very chaotic wind movement and the rest of the days, was a mobile Human frying pan. Enough of the Sun, I know, but if I could, I would write a whole post about it. Just to show its intensity. I still relied on rations of my loaves for days (morning-and-evening, unhealthy I know, but I had jacked up my mind to "survival-mode", so at that point, doing anything to survive was my only goal); didn't want to be forced to go out of my way, yet, and I was strongly advised against getting the provided food, and trust me, I did avoid such, through out, the only exceptions been, I did engage with their loaves and eggs, and for one day - spent in the kitchen (semi-voluntarily), I had a bit of their meat (or turkey, or chicken, can't honestly remeber what it was).

### A week in, already?

Building up to a routine - wake at 4, have my bath and some personal time, try to get some sleep back (on many days, unfortunately, couldn't) get called for a general devotion, some assembling and announcement, some moral lessons talk and all that, morning drills followed, and of course, DO NOT FORGET an important guy, take a guess... ### Two weeks in, oh no!

It felt like it came in probably earlier – way earlier, I was on my way as usual to fill up a bucket - power out (to drive home some irritating fact(s), there was barely power in such areas, on most days there was probably just ~2 hours of power - which was only enjoyable for a couple of minutes, because, there were of course "compulsory" activities to attend to, and the rest of the time? complete darkness, yes you heard me right COMPLETE DARKNESS; a far worse fast would be, the power was basically useless for the most part, with the lights as an exception - you would have to pay some vendor to help recharge your devices, there were no fans in the sleeping area until much later - the third week, and on and on and...) - I in a pressure of time, thought, "let's just rugged this, and jog to the tap to get water", okay maybe I ran, with no lights, trusting my instincts, and the question, at what point do we leap? leap? you might ask, yes, leap - over the open gutters (water channels), yes, not a single gutter was covered, and in my instinct, predicted at what point I need to take the jump, calculating wrongly, I fell, if it had just been a fall, great, I would be on my way, some part of my bucket and body made contact the the edge of the open gutter (an expression of a ...
